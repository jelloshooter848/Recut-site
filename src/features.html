Features

ReCut works like a familiar desktop editor: Source and Program monitors, a multi-track timeline and three-point editing. On top of that it adds tools for working across a whole franchise. This page describes ReCut {{featuresAsOf}}; the changelog lists what changed since.

Editing core

  • Source and Program monitors with JKL shuttle (up to 8x), frame stepping, In/Out marks and typed timecode entry.
  • Three-point Insert and Overwrite that honour sequence and source In/Out. On the first edit into an empty sequence, ReCut offers to match the sequence to the clip.
  • A multi-track timeline (video and audio tracks, linked audio and video, source patching, lock, mute, solo), snapping, Lift, Extract, Ripple Delete, Add Edit, Ripple Trim to playhead, nudge, copy and paste.
  • Selection, Track Select, Ripple, Rolling, Slip, Slide, Razor and Hand tools. Every edit is frame-exact.
  • Out-of-sync badges on linked clips, a stereo peak meter on the Program monitor, and a History panel with 200 undo steps.
  • Cross Dissolve, Dip to Black and Audio Crossfade transitions.
  • Clip speed from 1 % to 10 000 %, position, scale, rotation, opacity and crop, clip gain, level and fades, and track volume.
  • Keyframes for position, scale, opacity and volume, Linear or Ease, shown as diamonds on the timeline. They stay with the picture through trims, splits, rolls and slides, and the export matches the preview.
  • Nested sequences and compound clips. Make Compound Clip folds a selection into its own sequence, and edits to it show everywhere it is used. Drag a sequence onto the timeline to nest it; Break Apart reverses it.

Fan-edit tools

  • Series, season and franchise organisation. ReCut reads S01E03, 1x03 or “Season 1 Episode 3” on import and routes files into TV › Series › Season bins. Movies go to the Movies bin, and you can set a collection or franchise.
  • Scene detection with an adjustable threshold, and merge, split and tag of detected scenes.
  • Transcript search across a franchise. Import SRT or WebVTT (sidecars next to a video are picked up automatically) or extract embedded text subtitles. Search by project, series, season, franchise, collection, source or sequence, with regex and whole-word options, and click a hit to load it in the Source monitor at that line.
  • Speech-to-text (Whisper). Media without subtitles gets a searchable transcript from the built-in whisper.cpp engine, entirely offline. Models (tiny to large-v3-turbo) are downloaded once, when you choose one, and checked against a fixed checksum.
  • OCR for Blu-ray and DVD subtitles. Picture subtitles (PGS, VobSub, DVB, XSUB) are read into searchable text tracks by a built-in OCR engine (Tesseract). Languages are installed from inside ReCut; OCR then runs offline.
  • Scene library. Reusable source ranges with characters, location, arc, tags, rating and colour. Filter, sort and group them, and drag them to the timeline.
  • Story tagging and what-if experiments. Tag clips with characters, plotlines, locations and tags. The Storyline panel can highlight or solo matching clips, and its What if buttons switch matching or non-matching clips off and estimate the runtime. Remove disabled turns the experiment into the real cut (ripple, one undo step), and Duplicate as cut does the same in a new version of the sequence.
  • Storyline blocks. Label acts and arcs as coloured blocks above the cut.
  • Alternate cuts and snapshots. Duplicate a sequence as a versioned alternate cut, or take restorable snapshots.
  • Compare cuts. Play two sequences side by side on one clock and read a structural diff (same, moved, trimmed, only in A, only in B).
  • Continuity notes pinned to clips, which can be resolved, are gathered across sequences and can be copied as text.
  • Original source timecode. The Inspector shows the source file's timecode at the playhead, the Program timecode can switch from sequence to source, and Match Frame jumps to the source.
  • Subtitles that follow clips. Cues carried in from the source stay attached to their clip through moves, trims, ripples and speed changes.

Media and audio

  • Proxies. Media the preview cannot decode (HEVC, AC-3, DTS, MPEG-TS and more) gets a 540p H.264 proxy automatically, carrying every audio stream. Proxies are only for preview: export always reads the originals.
  • Stills in PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF and BMP are drawn directly; other formats FFmpeg decodes (TIFF, TGA, EXR, PSD, JPEG XL, AVIF, HEIC and more) are previewed from a PNG made on import. See FORMATS for the full list.
  • Relink with offline detection, a folder search that matches by name and size, and per-file Locate.
  • 5.1 audio. Export 5.1 sources as 5.1 AC-3 or a stereo downmix. Per clip, play one source channel as mono or a downmix with your own centre and surround levels, and Extract Centre Channel (Dialogue) puts the centre channel on its own linked clip.
  • Collect Project copies the project and the media it uses into one folder, verified, so it moves to another drive or machine intact.
  • Autosave, crash recovery, atomic saves with a backup copy, and a JSON project format. Every release opens projects saved by earlier releases from 0.3.0 on.

Export

  • MP4 with H.264 or H.265.
  • MKV with any number of audio tracks (for example 5.1 plus a stereo downmix, or a commentary track), soft subtitle tracks and chapters.
  • MOV with ProRes or DNxHR, for grading and finishing elsewhere.
  • WAV and FLAC audio only, as the mix or one file per track.
  • Presets for each, CRF or target bitrate, In to Out range, an FFmpeg command preview, a Checks list that warns before you export, and chunked rendering for very long timelines. Exports are frame-exact.
  • Subtitles export as SRT or VTT, or are burned in.

  • File › Export Timeline… writes a sequence as FCPXML, OpenTimelineIO or a CMX3600 EDL for DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro and other editors that read these formats.
  • The timeline links to your original media files; it carries no media, so the other editor needs the same files (or relinks them).
  • What the other format cannot represent (some keyframes, effects, nested sequences, subtitles) is approximated or left out, and the dialog's report lists it before saving.
  • Export only: importing FCPXML, OTIO or EDL is planned after 1.0. There is no AAF or Premiere XML.

Offline by design

ReCut works offline, with no account. It uses the network only for three things, each of which you start or allow yourself:

  • installing an OCR language (one file, checked against a fixed checksum);
  • installing a Whisper model (one file, checked the same way);
  • the update check, only if you agree to it or choose Help › Check for Updates. It tells you a new release exists; it never downloads or installs anything by itself.

Both downloads also have an Install from file… option for machines without network access.
