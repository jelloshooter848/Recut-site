ReCut

The free video editor made for fan edits.

Recuts, trilogy merges, character-focused cuts and “what if this subplot were gone” experiments. Find any line of dialogue across a whole franchise, build alternate cuts and compare them, with a real multi-track editor underneath. Everything runs on your computer: no account, no cloud, no upload.

What fan editors get

Find any line across a franchise

  • On import, ReCut reads S01E03, 1x03 or “Season 1 Episode 3” and files episodes into Series › Season bins. Movies go to their own bin, and you can set a franchise or collection.
  • Import SRT or WebVTT (sidecar files next to a video are picked up automatically), extract embedded text subtitles, transcribe speech with Whisper, or read Blu-ray and DVD picture subtitles with OCR.
  • Search by project, series, season, franchise, collection, source or sequence, with regex and whole-word options. Click a hit to open it in the Source monitor at that line.
  • Keep a scene library of reusable ranges with characters, location, arc, tags, rating and colour.

Build alternate cuts

  • Tag clips with characters, plotlines, locations and tags. The Storyline panel can highlight or solo matching clips.
  • What if switches matching clips off and estimates the new runtime. Remove disabled turns the experiment into the real cut in one undo step, or Duplicate as cut does it in a new version of the sequence.
  • Label acts and arcs as coloured storyline blocks, duplicate a sequence as a versioned alternate cut, or take restorable snapshots.
  • Always know where a frame came from: the Inspector shows the original source timecode at the playhead, and Match Frame jumps to the source.

Compare cuts

  • Play two sequences side by side on one clock.
  • Read a structural diff of every clip: same, moved, trimmed, only in A, only in B.
  • Pin continuity notes to clips, resolve them, and see them gathered across all your sequences.

Deliver it your way

  • MP4 (H.264 or H.265) for sharing; MKV with any number of audio tracks (for example 5.1 plus a stereo downmix, or a commentary track), soft subtitles and chapters.
  • MOV with ProRes or DNxHR for grading and finishing elsewhere, and WAV or FLAC audio only, as the mix or one file per track.
  • 5.1 sources export as 5.1 AC-3 or a stereo downmix, and Extract Centre Channel puts the centre (mostly dialogue) on its own clip.
  • Subtitles follow their clips through every edit and export as SRT or VTT, or burned in. A Checks list warns before you export, and exports are frame-exact.

Hand off to Resolve, Premiere or Final Cut Pro {{#if interchangeReleased}}New in {{interchangeVersion}}{{else}}Coming in {{interchangeVersion}}{{/if}}

  • File › Export Timeline… writes your sequence as FCPXML, OpenTimelineIO or a CMX3600 EDL, linked to your original media files, for DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro and other editors that read these formats.
  • Before saving, a report lists anything the other format cannot hold, so nothing is dropped silently.
  • Export only for now: importing timelines is planned after 1.0.

macOS

Apple Silicon (M1 or newer) Intel Mac

ReCut-<version>-macos-arm64.dmg

  • macOS 12 Monterey or newer. Not sure which? Apple menu › About This Mac: “Chip: Apple M…” means Apple Silicon, “Processor: Intel” means Intel.
  • Open the dmg and drag ReCut to Applications. The dmgs are signed and notarized by Apple, so ReCut opens normally.
  • On Intel Macs speech-to-text runs on the CPU only, so it is slower. Intel support may be retired after 1.0.

Linux

Download AppImage

ReCut-<version>-linux-x86_64.AppImage

  • x86-64 only. Make it executable and run it: chmod +x ReCut-*.AppImage
    ./ReCut-*.AppImage
  • If it says FUSE is missing, install libfuse2 (libfuse2t64 on Ubuntu 24.04 and later, fuse-libs on Fedora) or run it with --appimage-extract-and-run.
  • Needs glibc 2.28 or newer (Debian 10, Ubuntu 18.10, RHEL 8 or later).

Questions

Is ReCut free?

Yes. ReCut is free and open source. There is no account and no subscription, and nothing is uploaded: everything runs on your computer.

Which licence is it under?

ReCut is licensed under the MIT License. The release builds bundle FFmpeg, which ReCut runs as a separate program and which is licensed under the GPL version 3 or later; whisper.cpp, Tesseract, Electron and a few other components keep their own licences. See the third-party notices.

What is a fan edit?

A new cut of an existing film or series made by a fan: a tighter version, a trilogy merged into one film, a cut that follows a single character, or the story with a subplot taken out. ReCut is built around that kind of work: finding material across many films or episodes, trying alternate versions and comparing them.

Is it legal to use ReCut?

ReCut is an editing tool. It edits media files you supply. It does not download, rip or stream content, and it does not decrypt or get around DRM or any other copy protection. You are responsible for having the rights to the material you edit and for how you share the result. Copyright law differs from country to country, so respect copyright and the laws where you live. This is not legal advice.

What do I need to run it?
  • Windows: 64-bit (x64).
  • macOS: macOS 12 Monterey or newer, on Apple Silicon or Intel.
  • Linux: x86-64 with glibc 2.28 or newer, and the FUSE 2 library (or run the AppImage with --appimage-extract-and-run).

FFmpeg, the speech-to-text engine and the OCR engine are bundled. Speech-to-text runs on the CPU (with Metal on Apple Silicon), and the larger models are slow. ReCut works offline; it only goes online when you install a Whisper model or OCR language, or if you turn on the update check.

What can't it do yet?

Among other things: there is no colour grading, no titles or generators, three transitions (Cross Dissolve, Dip to Black, Audio Crossfade), no audio mixer or panning, and no automatic updates. {{#unless interchangeReleased}}Timeline export to other editors arrives in {{interchangeVersion}}.{{/unless}} The full, honest list is in LIMITATIONS, and what is planned is in the roadmap.
